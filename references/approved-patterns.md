# Approved Patterns

These are conditional examples, not new rules or fixed templates. Apply only when the source structure matches, and evaluate them under the referenced rule IDs.

## Symmetric ornament or flower

Use paired counterpart regions and reserve the centerline or paired small regions for unavoidable count imbalance. A symmetric contour may contain several four-direction pieces when they form one readable eight-direction structure. References: R3, R4, R9, R10.

## Near-fit regional coverage

When a target inventory nearly matches a legal source-region capacity, use it as a main direction but keep roughly 5-15% available when that preserves contour, symmetry, or hierarchy. A historical 206-to-214 fit is an example of a strong direction rather than a requirement for full coverage. References: R3, R6, R9.

## Reciprocal area exchange

Two similarly sized source regions can exchange their target colors to create a readable cleanup dependency. Use this only after checking the pair in the active profile order. References: R3, R5, R6, R11.

## Dark structural skeleton

A dark structural color may appear as several four-direction components when those pieces form one intentional eight-direction skeleton centered on the image or aligned to its internal structure. References: R4, R9, R10.

## Multi-segment boundary contour

Several four-direction boundary segments can be accepted when they combine into one clear eight-direction contour and remain visually ordered around the silhouette. References: R8, R10.

## Small near-hue repair

A minor near-hue placement can be accepted when it is grouped, paired or symmetric, and limited to local count repair with recorded evidence. References: R6, R9, R10.

## Small accent enclosure

A rare light, pink, magenta, or other accent inventory can form a compact highlight, rim, enclosure, or partial outline around one meaningful detail. References: R4, R9, R10.

For every pattern, record the affected regions, rule IDs, and why the pattern fits this source. Do not copy coordinates or color mappings from a historical example.
